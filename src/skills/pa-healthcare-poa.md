## Statutory Basis
Pennsylvania Health Care Agents and Representatives Act
20 Pa. C.S. §§5421-5461 (effective January 29, 2007)

## What This Document Does
Designates a health care agent to make health care decisions when principal
lacks capacity. Pennsylvania's equivalent of Florida's Health Care Surrogate.
May include a living will / advance directive declaration.

## Execution Requirements — CRITICAL (§5422)
- Principal signature (or direction if unable to sign)
- TWO witnesses:
  * Cannot be the health care agent
  * Cannot be the agent's spouse, parent, child, sibling, or presumptive heir
  * Cannot be principal's treating physician or employee of treating facility
  * Cannot be anyone who will inherit from the principal
  * Each witness: signature + printed name
- NO notary required by statute — but recommended
- Must be dated

## Statutory Notice to Principal
The following notice must appear before the signature:
"NOTICE TO THE PRINCIPAL: As the Principal, you have the right to make decisions
about your health care. No health care may be given to you over your objection,
and necessary health care may not be stopped or withheld if you object..."

## Agent's Authority
Standard authority includes:
- Consent to, refuse, or withdraw any health care
- Access medical records and information
- Employ/discharge health care providers
- Admit to or discharge from health care facilities
- Make anatomical gifts (if authorized)

## Living Will / Advance Directive Provisions
Address the following scenarios:
- Terminal illness (no reasonable expectation of recovery)
- Permanent unconsciousness / persistent vegetative state
- End-stage condition
For each: withhold/withdraw life-sustaining treatment? Artificially supplied nutrition?

## Out-of-Hospital DNR
Pennsylvania has a separate POLST (Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment)
form — the HC POA alone does not constitute a DNR order. Flag if client wants DNR.

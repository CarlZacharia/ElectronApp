## Statutory Basis
Pennsylvania Uniform Trust Act — 20 Pa. C.S. §§7701-7799.3
Effective November 6, 2006

## Purpose and Pennsylvania-Specific Considerations
- Avoids Pennsylvania probate (which can be slow and expensive)
- Avoids Pennsylvania Inheritance Tax on trust assets? NO —
  PA Inheritance Tax applies to trust assets just as it does to probate assets
  (tax is on the transfer, not the administration method)
- Provides incapacity management
- Multi-state property: avoids ancillary probate in other states

## Pennsylvania Inheritance Tax — CRITICAL
Always advise client:
- PA Inheritance Tax rates (current):
  * Spouse: 0%
  * Children/grandchildren: 4.5%
  * Siblings: 12%
  * Others: 15%
  * Charities: 0%
- Tax is assessed on FAIR MARKET VALUE at date of death
- Trust does NOT reduce PA inheritance tax — this is a common misconception
- Jointly held property with right of survivorship: 50% taxable to survivor

## Trustee Succession
1. Grantor/Trustee (during life and competency)
2. Successor Trustee (incapacity or death)
3. Second Successor Trustee (backup)

## Incapacity Standard
Specify how incapacity is determined:
- Certificate of physician(s) — recommend two physicians
- Court adjudication
- Combination

## Pour-Over Will
Must be executed simultaneously. Pennsylvania will requirements:
- Testator signature
- TWO witnesses (no notary required, but self-proving affidavit recommended)
- Self-proving affidavit: testator + witnesses before notary

## Trust Funding
Assets to transfer to trust:
- Real property: new deed required (no Lady Bird deed in PA — use regular deed)
- Bank/investment accounts: retitle
- Business interests: assignment or new operating agreement

Note: PA does NOT have a Lady Bird deed equivalent. Real property transfers
to trust require a standard deed — triggers deed transfer tax in some counties.
Check with local recorder of deeds.
